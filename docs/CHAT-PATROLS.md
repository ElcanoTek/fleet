# In-Chat Patrols & Timers (`chat_patrol`)

**Status:** shipped 2026-10-28.

## Overview

Traders and operators often want their assistant to stay on patrol inside an active chat conversation — checking deal pacing, margin anomalies, backfills, or refreshing Pages dashboards every few hours (or waking up after a one-shot delay) with the conversation's full transcript, workspace files, Python variables, and enabled MCP connectors preserved.

Previously, recurring work required promoting a conversation to a standalone headless job in the Operations Center (`schedule_task`), where each occurrence starts in a fresh transcript outside the chat thread. In-chat patrols (`chat_patrol`) complement Operations Center tasks by scheduling recurring cron turns or one-shot timers directly on a `conversations` row.

## Shipped Architecture

1. **Persistence and firing (`sched` migrations `076_chat_schedules` and `077_move_chat_schedules`, `087_input_queue_patrol_mode.sql`, `088_input_queue_patrol_mode_validate.sql`, `internal/store/chat_schedules.go`, `internal/sched/db/chat_schedules.go`, `internal/sched/scheduler/chat_schedules.go`)**
   - Since #2049 M5 ([ADR-0104](adr/0104-one-scheduler.md)) the rows live in the scheduler's schema (`sched.chat_schedules`, moved from `chat.chat_schedules` by sched migration 077) and are fired by the scheduler's one tick loop, not a chat-side sweeper.
   - `chat_schedules` stores per-conversation recurring cron patrols (`cron_expr` with `MinChatPatrolCronInterval = 5m`, validated across 48 consecutive firings) and one-shot delay timers (`duration_seconds` between 10s and 30d), up to `MaxActiveSchedulesPerConversation = 5`.
   - Creating a recurring cron patrol on a conversation that already has an active or paused recurring patrol updates the existing patrol in place by default (`AllowMultiple = false`), preventing accidental duplicate patrols when adjusting cadence or instructions.
   - `SweepExpired` exempts conversations that hold an active or paused schedule in `chat_schedules` so long-running patrol threads are never reaped while active.
   - Archiving a conversation (`store.SetArchived`) pauses every active patrol and timer in it, in the same transaction: a fire in flight is revoked, the schedules' queued turns are cancelled, and a UI-only notice says how many were paused. Nobody reads an archived chat, so its schedules would only spend. They stay paused on unarchive, and a schedule cannot be created or resumed while the chat is archived (`unarchive it before starting a patrol or timer`).
   - Each fire is claim → delivery → settle. The scheduler leases a due row (`lease_owner` / `lease_expires_at`, the tasks' lease model); the chat server's `DeliverChatScheduleFire` enqueues a `chat_input_queue` row with server-owned `mode = 'patrol'` (`store.InputModePatrol`) under the idempotency key `chat-patrol:<id>:<run>:<s|m>-<fire_id>` (s scheduled, m Run now). Patrol provenance is the queue row's server-owned `mode = 'patrol'` in the schedule's conversation, never the caller-supplied key alone: a user input that borrows a `chat-patrol:` key neither blocks nor counts as a patrol turn, and isn't cancelled with the schedule; the settle advances `run_count` / `next_run_at` / `status`, releases the lease and, for a fire that ran (`queued`) or was turned away (`busy`, `gone`), appends a row to `sched.chat_schedule_runs` (the run history, pruned with task runs by `FLEET_RUN_LOG_RETENTION_DAYS`); a Run now that coalesces onto a turn in flight (409) or is refused on a full queue (429) adds none. A fire that dies before it settles (or whose delivery failed, after a backoff) is re-claimed as itself by the recovery pass when its lease expires, whatever the schedule's status, and its re-delivery finds its own queue row. Pausing or cancelling a schedule revokes a fire in flight. Deleting a conversation deletes its schedules and their run history: a trigger on `chat.conversations` that sched migration 077 creates (and its down removes). On a database the scheduler initialised first, 077 found no chat schema, so `fleet serve` installs the trigger once both schemas exist — retrying in the background (5s doubling to 5m) for as long as the process runs if that fails — and the install removes every schedule orphaned until then. Once the trigger is in, deleting a conversation never leaves its schedules behind; until it is in, orphaned rows are inert (a fire of one finds its conversation gone and cancels it) and are removed by the install. **Run now** takes the same path through `Scheduler.RunChatScheduleNow`. If the conversation already has a queued or running patrol turn of the owner's, or a full input queue, due schedules coalesce and advance `next_run_at` (recurring patrols to the next cron tick; one-shot timers by 30s) so blocked schedules never pile up or starve later conversations. A scheduled fire turned away this way records why on its `busy` history row (`reason`, migration 0203: `patrol_in_flight` or `queue_full`; `''` for any other outcome and for rows recorded before 0203).
   - **A waiting approval card does not hold a scheduled fire** ([ADR-0120](adr/0120-a-waiting-approval-card-does-not-hold-a-scheduled-run.md)). Until 2026-11-24 it did: a fire that found a pending card of the owner's (other than an email preview or a model suggestion) was turned away as `busy`, reason `approval_pending`, and a recurring patrol's slot was not run later. That was deliberate (a patrol never stacked turns behind an unanswered card), and it cost whole runs to protect one card: a once-daily 6:30 patrol would have lost the next morning's run to a card for an unrelated change a trader asked for the afternoon before (a card waits 24 hours by default); a daily 8:00 report was skipped behind a card its own previous run had staged, which timed out nine minutes after the slot; and a two-hourly patrol lost three slots in a row to one card (2026-11-18). `DeliverChatScheduleFire` no longer looks for a pending card, for a patrol or a timer, in any approval mode (all it still does with `chat.approvals` is supersede a pending model suggestion when it queues a scheduled turn, as before). The card is not touched by the fire: it stays pending, keeps its `expires_at` and its resume arming, and is approved, declined or timed out exactly as before. `approval_pending` is still a valid `reason` (0203's CHECK, `models.ChatScheduleBusyApprovalPending`) because run-history rows recorded before this carry it, and **Recent runs** still reads them ("Skipped: an approval was waiting"); nothing writes it any more.

2. **Turn Execution, Approvals & Python State (`internal/httpapi/`, `internal/tools/`)**
   - Draining a `mode = 'patrol'` queue row launches a governed chat turn with `inputKind = agent.InputKindChatPatrol` (`"chat_patrol"`), rendered in the transcript as a collapsible `PatrolTurnNotice` card rather than a user bubble.
   - The interactive `chat_patrol` tool (`create`, `list`, `update`, `pause`, `resume`, `cancel`) lets the assistant manage the current conversation's schedule in-turn and emits `patrol.updated` SSE events so the UI updates live.
   - Model-callable `chat_patrol` never elevates `conv.AutoApprove` without explicit user consent; only user-authenticated HTTP requests (`POST /api/conversations/{id}/schedules` or `PATCH /api/conversations/{id}`) can enable `conv.AutoApprove`.
   - A patrol turn keeps `show_ui`, so it can draw cards and refresh [dashboard](DASHBOARD-VIEW.md) panels; in a multiplayer chat every participant sees them. When the chat has board cards, the turn's injected context lists them (title, card id, panel key and how to update each), so a run updates the existing panels instead of adding new ones even after a compaction; in a multiplayer chat its replay is the whole chat's history, other participants' turns included. A patrol turn never arms the Autonomous 10-second auto-submit countdown on a card it shows, and it is not offered `enable_mcp_connectors`.
   - When a patrol turn stages a critical tool approval card while `conv.AutoApprove` is off, the card receives at least a 24-hour (`86400s`) expiry floor and automatically arms `resume_after_approval`, so approving the card later resumes the patrol conversation (once no other card of that person's is still pending in the chat: [RESUME-AFTER-APPROVAL.md](RESUME-AFTER-APPROVAL.md)).
   - **A patrol turn is told which approval cards are waiting** (`appendWaitingApprovalsBlock`, `internal/httpapi/chat_approvals_context.go`), because it now runs while they wait. The turn's injected context gets one block listing the pending cards of the person the turn runs as (the schedule's owner), oldest first: the tool, one summary line (the title and subtitle of the card's [describer card](APPROVAL-CARD-DESCRIBERS.md) when it has one, else its top-level arguments as `key=value`), the approval id, when it was staged and when it expires, in UTC. The block says none of them has run, tells the run not to stage or run the same action again and to report them as waiting rather than done, and says that a new card for one of those tools replaces the waiting card for it: so the run is to use such a tool only to bring an earlier scheduled run's own card up to date, never to replace a card a person asked for (it leaves the tool for a later run and says what is waiting), and to say which card it replaced when it did. A `schedule_task` card's line says the opposite, because a new `schedule_task` call is never a replacement (each proposes a task of its own, `supersedesEarlierCards`): calling it again would leave two cards for one task. It is bounded like the board list: at most 12 cards one per line (the rest counted by tool, at most 8 tools named), 200 runes per summary, 60 per argument value, with brackets and control characters removed so nothing a card carries can close the block. A card whose arguments the secret redaction would alter is named by tool only. Not listed: an email preview (display-only), a model suggestion (superseded when the patrol turn is queued), a card already past its deadline, and other participants' cards (they never held this person's patrol, and a card this turn stages cannot replace them). A turn a person typed and an approval-resume turn get no block. If the pending cards cannot be read the turn is not started without the list: the queued row goes back to the queue and is tried again, as for a failed history read.
   - **What a patrol turn's own calls do to a waiting card** is the stager's ordinary behaviour, unchanged, which is why the turn is told. If the turn stages a card for a tool (any but `schedule_task`) that already has a pending card of the same person's from an earlier turn, the earlier card is superseded (rejected, "Superseded by a newer call to this tool.", never resumed) and the tool result tells the model how many cards it replaced (`SupersededCount`). This is the rule every later turn has always followed, and it is the right one for a patrol's own cards: a daily report's card from yesterday is replaced by today's instead of stacking one stale card per run. For a card a person asked for with the same tool it means a run could replace it with nobody there to see; fleet does not record which kind of turn staged a card, so this is not enforced: the block tells the run to leave that tool alone for this run, and the tool result and the superseded card both say so if it does not. Cards for other tools, and every other participant's cards, are left alone. In Auto-approve or Autonomous a call the mode covers runs without a card, so nothing is staged and nothing is superseded: a waiting card for the same tool stays pending, and repeating its action would run it now and again on approval, which the block also tells the run not to do.
   - **A card settled while a patrol turn is queued or running** follows the resume rules as they are: an approved or declined armed card is claimed into one `resume` queue row behind the patrol turn (it never runs beside it and is claimed once), and a card that times out starts no turn in a chat that has a schedule (`ApprovalResumeSkipTimedOut`: the next run picks the work up). Either way the outcome is written into the conversation as it always is, and the patrol turn in flight is not interrupted.
   - `python_bridge.py` automatically checkpoints and restores serializable user variables (`.fleet_python_state.pkl` in the conversation workspace) across turns and patrol runs, and persistent sandboxes scope their writable bind-mount to `<WorkspaceHostDir>/<convID>` and reap any detached non-kernel processes on turn release.

3. **Web UI (`PatrolBanner.tsx`, `PatrolsDialog.tsx`, `patrolBarPrefs.ts`, `PatrolSchedulePicker.tsx`, `patrolSchedule.ts`, `Composer.tsx`, `chat-experience.tsx`)**
   - **Patrols & timers** (`PatrolsDialog`) is where the chat page creates, edits and stops a patrol (the assistant's `chat_patrol` tool, the Patrol Launchpad and persona patrols still can too). **More options → Patrol & schedule** opens it, and so does the composer's clock once the chat has a patrol. With no patrol yet it opens straight on the **New patrol or timer** form (`PatrolForm`); otherwise it lists every armed or paused schedule with **Run now** and **Pause/Resume**, and a card expands to the approval mode, the run prompt, **Edit schedule & task** (the same `PatrolForm`, sent as `PATCH …/schedules/<sid>` with only the changed fields, so a label or prompt edit never re-arms the schedule) and **Remove patrol** / **Cancel timer**, which needs a second, confirming click. The form's presets are `Every 1h`, `Every 2h`, `Every 4h`, `Every 6h`, `Weekdays 9am`, `Daily 9am` and `Custom` under **Repeats**, and `In 30m`, `In 2h` and `Custom` under **Once (timer)**. A recurring `Custom` opens the visual schedule builder (`Every N hours`, `Every N minutes`, `Every day`, `Weekdays (Mon–Fri)`, `Specific days of the week`, time picker, IANA time-zone dropdown with `Custom zone`, live `describeCronExpression` preview, `Advanced cron` input). A timer `Custom` takes minutes or hours (up to 1440m / 168h).
   - The **Patrol bar** (`PatrolBanner`) sits above the composer: human-readable cadence (`formatScheduleCadence`), live countdown, run count, an approval-mode dropdown (switching an armed patrol to Autonomous asks once more inside the menu, before the Autonomous risk prompt), **Run now**, **Pause/Resume** and **Hide**. The bar has no stop control. Its old `×` cancelled the patrol (a `DELETE`), and a Raptive user clicked it expecting it to dismiss the bar (2026-11-17), so stopping moved into the dialog behind a confirm.
   - **Hide** folds the bar into the composer's clock (ringed while hidden, with a six-second **Patrol bar hidden · Undo** note). The choice is per browser and per conversation (`patrolBarPrefs`, guarded `localStorage`), not stored on the server, so another device still shows the bar. A hidden bar comes back while the conversation has a pending approval card, with **Review** to scroll to it, and the clock turns amber. Approvals are per conversation, so the note is shown on the schedule that ran last. The dialog's **Show bar above composer** switch shows a hidden bar again.
   - **The "Patrol on hold" note is gone** (it shipped on 2026-11-18 and was removed with the hold itself, ADR-0120). It told an owner that their patrol's slots were being skipped behind a waiting approval card. No slot is skipped for a card any more, so nothing could show it: `PatrolSkippedNote`, the Patrols page's "runs skipped · approval waiting" chip, the `skipped` field on the schedules and patrols responses and the store reads behind it (`skippedForApproval`, `PatrolSkips`) were deleted rather than left unreachable. A box that still has `approval_pending` rows in its run history shows them where they belong, in a patrol's **Recent runs**. One part of that change stays, for the slots that are still turned away (a patrol turn in flight, a full queue): such a slot queues no turn, so nothing streams to an open chat, and the chat page re-reads `GET …/schedules` 20s after the open chat's next active slot (every minute while that slot is already past) so the bar's countdown moves on instead of sticking at "due now".
   - The approval mode is the conversation's (`approval_mode`), not per schedule, so the bar shows the dropdown once and the dialog notes that it applies to every patrol in the chat.
   - The page polls while a patrol is active, so background patrol turns stream into open tabs automatically.

## Deferred Scope

- Cross-conversation patrol dashboards (listing all active chat patrols across every conversation on one admin screen) are deferred; Operations Center remains the board for fleet-wide scheduled jobs.
